## Request (10-16, 23:20)

"할수 있으면 진짜 좋겠다 컴퓨터에서 거의 모두 분석해서 들어가게" — analyse nearly every
track on the computer and put the SensMe analysis into the files, so the Walkman shows
channels for music Music Center for PC never saw.

Two halves: an analyser that turns PCM into the SMFMF chunks, and a writer that puts a
block into a file without harming anything else in it. The writer is done; the analyser
is being reverse engineered.

## Writing the block into a file

`tags::write_smfmf` (b37, 23:48) first went through lofty like the tag editor. Run over
75 tracks pulled from the ZX507 (60 MP3, 15 FLAC) with the frame-loss check, it lost
data the check caught and some it did not:

- 2 files: "writing would have lost the TSOA, TSOT frame(s)" — lofty's v2.3 writer drops
  frames it considers v2.4-only. 1 file: TDRC, TDOR.
- 2 files: "failed to parse file: no format could be determined" from lofty.
- TYER `20080711` (2AM): read as a timestamp, unparseable, dropped.
- 4 files: USLT lyrics changed (lofty strips the doubled BOM).

The tag editor from 44fc0fd has the same path, so the same batch was re-tagged with
`album=Test Album` (b44): 2 files refused, and on 18 of the written ones fields were
silently lost — `date` 13, `ALBUM ARTIST` 4, `WRITER` 4, `encoder` 4, `Date`,
`TOTALTRACKS`, `TOTALDISCS` 1 each — from lofty's FLAC Vorbis split/merge and from
v1-only MP3s losing the year.

So MP3 and FLAC are now edited as bytes (`library::tagbytes`): only the ID3 tag or the
FLAC metadata blocks are rebuilt, frames and fields that are not edited are copied as they
are, padding absorbs the size change so the audio offset stays put, the audio is streamed
across, and the copy is read back before it replaces the file. lofty keeps MP4, Ogg, Opus
and the ID3 tags too odd to splice (unsync, extended header, footer), with dropped frames
put back afterwards. An MP3 with only ID3v1 gets a v2.3 tag that carries the v1 fields
as the same bytes: Android's stagefright reads v2 when it exists and never falls back to
v1, so a bare new v2 tag would hide the title.

CLI: `uwm sensme-copy <from> <to>` and `uwm tag <file> field=value...`.

## Running Sony's engine at native speed (12:16–13:40)

The machine rebooted at 12:16 and took the analyser agent and `/tmp` with it. Its transcript
showed where it had got to: the engine is `AVLib/MMLib11.dll`, a COM class (MusicAnalysis2)
that imports nothing of Gracenote's, and under the Unicorn emulator 20 of the 32 FLAC blocks
came out byte for byte, at 80–700 s a track.

- `mmhost.exe` (12:29, our own C, zig `cc` here, mingw on the burst runner) loads the DLL
  under Wine and takes jobs on stdin: about 1 s a track.
- 12:31: same answers as the emulator, so the misses were not emulation. Feeding the PCM in
  4096-byte `InputPCM` calls instead of one: 3 of 3, then 31 of 32 FLAC byte for byte. The
  one left is 88.2 kHz/24-bit; Music Center resampled it itself.
- MP3 (88 pulled read-only from the ZX507): 22 of 88 with ffmpeg's defaults. Without gapless
  trimming (`-flags2 +skip_manual`) 48 were exactly 1152 samples short: Music Center plays the
  Xing/Info frame as a frame of silence, but only when it directly follows the tag (all 8
  files with 166–187 junk bytes in between had it skipped). With both: 67–68 of 88.
- The rest differ mostly in STHF, and STHF moves with the piece size (4096, 2048, 4608 and
  9216 each fix different tracks; one track flipped between two full runs). The engine has a
  worker thread. Channels, which is what the player shows: 32 of 32 FLAC and 87 of 88 MP3
  agree with Music Center's blocks; the miss (79) was analysed when the file was 157 s longer.
